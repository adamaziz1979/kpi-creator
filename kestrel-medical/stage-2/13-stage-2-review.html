2. The calculations

Gross margin per cent (K1)

  • Result of the check on Gary's draft: Confirmed (Phase 2 KPI: placeholder tile on the dashboard)
  • Plain English: Net sales less cost of sales, over net sales.
  • Calculation: (SUM(net_value_aud) - SUM(cost_of_sales)) / SUM(net_value_aud)
  • Sources and why: sales_order_lines.net_value_aud (the sales system of record). Cost of sales: no field found. Phase 2: cost of sales per shipped order line from SAP product costing and billing documents, joined on order and line number.
  • Grain: Order line (each order has one line)
  • Filters: Shipped lines, January to December. Must reconcile to finance's 41% before use. Placeholder tile until then; the stated 41% may appear only as a text note on the placeholder, never in the headline slot and never on the stand-in.
  • Baseline: Phase 2 placeholder: waiting for cost of sales from finance. No baseline can be calculated from the Sample data: no cost of sales field exists in any of the 11 files. Finance's stated figure of about 41% is a text note on the placeholder only, not calculated. Period: Phase 2 (the stated figure is the current year, from the sales leader). Evidence: Working Hypothesis (a stated figure, not reproducible)
  • Pace needed: Not applicable until Phase 2: the placeholder shows no pace needed
  • Run rate: Not available: no history without cost of sales
  • Reading it: Phase 2: waiting for data. Cannot say whether on pace until cost of sales is supplied
  • Counter-metric: Net sales value year to date (Revenue growth on the year, K3), read alongside: a margin lift bought by dropping low-margin sales shows as falling net sales. Net sales Jan to Sep 2026 are $149.36m, -6.8% on the same months of 2025
  • Notes: Phase 2 KPI. Formula confirmed as the right shape. Data mapping NOT confirmed: no cost field (checked every column in all 11 files; challenge note C1). Phase 2 needs cost of sales per shipped order line from SAP product costing, joined on order and line number, reconciled to finance's 41% before switch-on. First pass: placeholder tile plus the stand-in Net price realisation (Adam's choice), which never carries this KPI's name, target or status colour.
  • Stand-in (first pass only, not the KPI): 87.36% of list price kept on shipped lines (net sales $149.36m), against 87.67% in the same months of 2025. By full year: 2021 89.72%, 2022 89.28%, 2023 89.28%, 2024 87.61%, 2025 87.22%. Excluding warranty replacement lines: 87.26% against 87.58%. Price side only: this is not gross margin. Period: January to September 2026 against the same months of 2025, ship date (Sample data). Evidence: Observed Fact for the calculation; Working Hypothesis that it moves with gross margin (no cost data to test the link). Calculation: SUM(net_value_aud) / SUM(list_price * qty), shipped lines, ship date

Average discount off list price on big deals (K2)

  • Result of the check on Gary's draft: Corrected
  • Plain English: One minus net value over list value, across big-deal lines, weighted by value.
  • Calculation: 1 - SUM(net_value_aud) / SUM(list_price * qty), order_type = tender_contract
  • Sources and why: sales_order_lines.list_price, .qty, .net_value_aud, .order_type, .order_date. discount_pct used as a cross-check only.
  • Grain: Order line
  • Filters: Order type tender_contract (placeholder until Sharon sets a size rule); period by order date; no lines excluded for missing list price (none).
  • Baseline: 17.03% value-weighted discount off list on tender contract order lines (5,485 lines). Placeholder big-deal definition. Period: January to September 2026, order date. Evidence: Observed Fact (calculation); Working Hypothesis for which orders count as big deals
  • Pace needed: -0.20 points a month: a straight-line path from 17.03% (Sep-26) to 14.0% (Dec-27) over 15 months. Path: Dec-26 16.42%, Jun-27 15.21%, Dec-27 14.00%
  • Run rate: 0.000 points a month over the last 3 months (flat)
  • Reading it: Green today (on the path at its starting point) but off pace: a flat discount goes Amber from Oct-26 and Red from Feb-27 (more than 1 point above the path). It has to fall about 0.20 points a month
  • Counter-metric: Tender contract net sales and the Big tender win rate (K4): pulling back discount can simply lose the deals. Tender contract net sales Jan to Sep: $86.7m in 2026 against $95.1m in 2025
  • Notes: Corrected (C4, accepted in v1.1): the draft placeholder 'orders linked to a contract_id' picks up service contract lines, not tenders. No order reaches $5m, so an order-total rule gives nothing. v1.1 changes the target to a path back towards about 14% (2023 level) by Dec-27, Gary's proposal for Sharon to confirm (C5). Discount recalculated from list_price, qty and net_value_aud agrees with discount_pct (99.9% of lines within 0.002 points). The 2026 reference is Jan to Sep until the year closes.

Target path (v1.1, Gary's proposal, for Sharon to confirm): straight line from 17.03% (Sep-26) to 14.0% (Dec-27), -0.20 points a month: Sep-26 17.03%, Dec-26 16.42%, Mar-27 15.82%, Jun-27 15.21%, Sep-27 14.61%, Dec-27 14.00%. Green at or below the path, Amber up to 1 point above, Red more than 1 point above. If the discount stays flat it is Amber from Oct-26 and Red from Feb-27.

Average discount on tender contract lines by year (order date): 2021: 14.04%, 2022: 14.04%, 2023: 14.05%, 2024: 17.03%, 2025: 17.03%, Jan to Sep 2026: 17.03%.

If 'big deal' meant something else (Jan to Sep 2026): tender contract and distributor lines 16.78%; the biggest 1% of lines by value ($87,101 or more) 4.14%. The baseline depends heavily on the definition, so Sharon's size rule (open question) matters.

Revenue growth on the year (K3)

  • Result of the check on Gary's draft: Confirmed
  • Plain English: Net sales shipped this year to date over net sales shipped in the same months last year, minus one.
  • Calculation: SUM(net_value_aud, ship month 1..m, 2026) / SUM(net_value_aud, ship month 1..m, 2025) - 1
  • Sources and why: sales_order_lines.net_value_aud and .actual_ship_date (the draft rule, until finance confirms its revenue rule); .order_type checked.
  • Grain: Order line
  • Filters: Shipped lines only; year to date against year to date.
  • Baseline: -6.8% ($149.36m against $160.26m). Full year 2025 on 2024 was +0.4%, which matches 'flat last year'. Period: January to September 2026 against January to September 2025, ship date. Evidence: Observed Fact
  • Pace needed: +0.72 points a month: from -6.8% to +4.0% over 15 months (Sep-26 to Dec-27). In v1.1 the 4% is measured on 2026 full-year actuals: about $203.5m in 2027 ($16.96m a month) against $16.6m a month so far in 2026
  • Run rate: +0.27 points a month over the last 3 months (year-to-date growth moved from -7.6% in Jun to -6.8% in Sep)
  • Reading it: Off pace: the run rate is about 2.7 times too slow. Even on the 2026 base, 2027 would be about 3.1% below 2025
  • Counter-metric: Value-weighted average discount across all lines (Average discount, K2 context): growth bought with price shows here. All lines Jan to Sep 2026: 12.52%, against 12.84% for the full year 2025
  • Notes: Confirmed on ship date. Numbers are unchanged from v1.0. Checked order types: there are no credit or return types; warranty replacement lines carry full list value at 0% discount ($1.8m in 2025) and removing them changes the baseline to -6.9%, so it is immaterial. On order date the baseline is -9.9%. 1,517 lines (1.26%, all 2026) have no ship date yet and are not counted. The 2027 measure cannot exist before 2027, so the 2026 year-to-date figure is the baseline. v1.1 sets the base for the 4% as 2026 full-year actuals (estimate $195.68m until the year closes); the base is still an open question for Sharon and finance (C6).

Year-to-date growth by month in 2026 (ship date): Jan -12.5%, Feb -9.1%, Mar -8.7%, Apr -7.8%, May -8.2%, Jun -7.6%, Jul -7.8%, Aug -7.3%, Sep -6.8%.

What 4% means on the v1.1 base (2026 full-year actuals; estimate, Working Hypothesis): full-year 2026 is estimated at $195.68m (Jan to Sep actual plus Q4 2025 moved by the year-to-date rate). 4% on that is $203.5m for 2027, which is -3.1% against full-year 2025 ($209.95m).

Big tender win rate (K4)

  • Result of the check on Gary's draft: Corrected
  • Plain English: Tenders won out of the last 5 decided tenders with a contract value of $5m or more (rolling five-tender window), with the calendar-year count alongside.
  • Calculation: COUNT(outcome = won) over the last 5 tenders with outcome IN (won, lost) and contract value >= 5,000,000, ordered by close_date
  • Sources and why: tender_register.outcome, .estimated_value, .contract_length, .close_date, .tender_reference. The bid manager's file, because the sales leader does not trust the CRM for win-loss.
  • Grain: Tender
  • Filters: Contract value $5m or more (annual amounts times years); exclude undecided, partial and cancelled; show the count next to the rate.
  • Baseline: 2 of the last 5 big tenders (40%). Calendar year alongside: 2024 1 of 2, 2025 0 of 2, 2026 to Sep 1 of 1. Won Coastal Ambulance Service ($14.0m) and Capital Region Health ($5.6m); lost Southern Metro Health Network ($6.5m), Western Plains LHD ($7.2m) and Highlands Ambulance ($11.8m). Period: The last five tenders of $5m or more in the register, closing March 2024 to February 2026. Evidence: Observed Fact (small numbers)
  • Pace needed: Target open (Q-1): Deb Harlow sets it. Against Gary's starting proposal of at least 3 of the last 5 by Dec-27, the next 2 big tenders must both be won: after one win the window still reads 2 of 5, after two it reads 3 of 5. At about 2 big tenders a year, about 2.5 are expected by Dec-27
  • Run rate: Not meaningful: only 5 events, so no run rate over 3 periods
  • Reading it: Not rated until Deb sets the target. If 3 of 5 is accepted it is reachable only if both of the next two big tenders are won, with little room for a loss
  • Counter-metric: Number and value of big tenders bid, and the discount on tender contract orders (Average discount, K2): a count can be lifted by bidding only on easy tenders or cutting price. Big tenders bid: 2 (2024), 2 (2025), 1 (2026 to Sep)

2. Problem Statement

Kestrel has lost three of its last five big tenders, each over five million dollars, and believes price is the reason but can't yet prove or disprove it. With gross margin at about 41 per cent against global's 43 per cent target for 2027, and thirty monthly reports that give different answers, the business needs one set of evidence before it asks global for more discount room.

The problem as stated

Sharon, in her words: lost three of the last five big tenders, each over five million dollars, "never happened to me in 22 years". Reps say Kestrel is being undercut 10 to 15 per cent. She wants a win-loss view and pricing against the competition, so she can take evidence to global and argue for more discount room. And she wants out of Excel: thirty monthly reports, and half of them say different things.

The problem as Gary understands it

  • The ask and the target pull against each other. The ask is evidence for more discount. Global's target is more margin (about 41 to 43 per cent in 2027) and 4 per cent revenue growth, after a flat year
  • The loss reasons are belief, not yet evidence. They come from reps and one procurement person. Sharon's own account of the three losses: one "was price" (Southern Metropolitan Health Network), one was "about the stretcher as much as anything" (Highlands Ambulance, a 15-year customer), and one went to a distributor that bundles several brands (Western Plains Health District, won by Allied Clinical Supply)
  • The evidence exists but hasn't been used. Deb Harlow, the bid manager, keeps the tender file and the debrief letters
  • Nobody agrees on the base numbers. Thirty monthly reports from ERP extracts disagree, and Sharon spends three days a month reconciling them

Gary's note: the question I'd put to Sharon is "What decision changes if this number moves?" Here, the answer is the discount case to global.
