1. What I propose

  • Placeholder tile for each of the four KPIs above, named exactly as in the Business Requirements, with the text "Phase 2: waiting for data" and the owner's role. No status colour, no number in the headline slot.
  • Substitute shown as a small, separate stand-in, titled with its own plain name, labelled "Sample data" and "Stand-in, not the KPI". It must never carry the real KPI's name, target, status colour or the sales leader's stated 41%.
  • Honest limits up front. For Gross margin per cent (K1) and Gross margin by product group (K1.1) the substitute is a price-side signal only. For the other two it is a closer relative of the real KPI.
  • Phase 2 gate. Each placeholder turns into the real KPI only when the data engineer's feed reconciles to the owner's figure (for K1, to finance's 41%).

2. The four KPIs, one by one

Gross margin per cent (K1)

  • Why it can't be built now: no cost, COGS, margin or standard cost field in any of the 11 files. The stated 41% can't be reproduced.
  • Substitute (plain name): Net price realisation.
  • Calculation: SUM(net_value_aud) / SUM(list_price * qty), shipped lines, by ship date, January to September.
  • Source fields: sales_order_lines.net_value_aud, .list_price, .qty, .actual_ship_date.
  • Baseline (Sample data): 87.36% for January to September 2026 (net sales $149.36m). Same months of 2025: 87.67%. By full year: 2021 89.72%, 2022 89.28%, 2023 89.28%, 2024 87.61%, 2025 87.22%. Excluding warranty replacement lines (full list value, $1.39m in 2026 to date): 87.26% against 87.58%. Monthly 2026: Jan 89.70, Feb 85.52, Mar 87.37, Apr 85.62, May 86.14, Jun 87.37, Jul 89.35, Aug 86.02, Sep 86.89.
  • Evidence label: Observed Fact for the calculation. Working Hypothesis that it moves with gross margin, because there is no cost data to test the link.
  • What it tells Adam: how much of list price Kestrel keeps after discounting. The step down in 2024 (89.28% to 87.61%) lines up with the 3-point rise in tender discount in the Calculation Pack (challenge note C5). A fall in realisation will pull margin down unless cost falls too.
  • What it doesn't tell Adam: gross margin. It ignores cost of sales entirely, so it can't say whether margin is 41%, 43% or anything else, and it can't be set against the 43% target or given a pace needed.
  • How it could mislead: (1) List prices can rise or fall, so realisation can move with no change in margin. (2) Product mix: Service, Parts and consumables carry little or no discount, so a shift in mix moves the total (Service fell from 16.7% to 14.2% of net sales, which alone nudges realisation down) without any price decision. (3) Realisation can be flat while cost rises. (4) Reading a rise as a margin gain would be wrong.
  • Recommendation: show it as a stand-in beside the placeholder. Never as a margin proxy with a target.

Gross margin by product group (K1.1)

  • Why it can't be built now: same missing cost of sales.
  • Substitute (plain name): Net price realisation by product group, with each group's share of net sales.
  • Calculation: as above, grouped by material_group.
  • Source fields: as above plus sales_order_lines.material_group.
  • Baseline (Sample data, Jan to Sep 2026, change from the same months of 2025 in points, share of net sales in brackets): Beds 84.33% (-0.34, 21.8%); Defibrillators 84.17% (+0.12, 19.6%); Defibrillator consumables 95.00% (0.00, 8.7%); ED stretchers 84.45% (+0.30, 7.1%); Loading systems 82.91% (+0.06, 8.4%); Stair chairs 83.10% (-0.08, 2.8%); Manual stretchers 83.12% (-0.15, 3.8%); Powered stretchers 82.95% (+0.01, 9.1%); Parts 100.00% (0.00, 4.4%); Service 100.00% (0.00, 14.2%).
  • Evidence label: Observed Fact for the calculation. Working Hypothesis that group margin follows group realisation.
  • What it tells Adam: which groups carry deep discounts. The equipment groups all keep about 83% to 84% of list, so no single group stands out on price. Within-group realisation barely moved (all within 0.4 points), so the small fall in total realisation is mostly mix, not price.
  • What it doesn't tell Adam: which groups make money. Service and Parts at 100% of list say nothing about their margin (service cost is labour, parts cost is purchase price), and equipment groups differ in cost.
  • How it could mislead: the 100% for Service and Parts looks like "best" but is only "no discounting". Ranking groups by realisation would send attention to the wrong place.
  • Recommendation: show as a stand-in table beside the placeholder, with the note on Service and Parts.

Monthly reports that match the agreed set of numbers (K5)

  • Why it can't be built now: finance hasn't defined the agreed set, so "match" has no test. The index holds what each owner says the report uses, one snapshot, no history, so no run rate.
  • Substitute (plain name): Reports on the draft agreed set, with the number of different combinations in use.
  • Calculation: COUNT(sales_definition = ship date AND customer_grouping = parent where known) / COUNT(reports), plus COUNT(DISTINCT sales_definition, customer_grouping). The draft set is the rule used for Revenue growth on the year (K3), labelled "draft" on the tile.
  • Source fields: monthly_reports_index.workbook, .sales_definition, .customer_grouping, .last_refreshed.
  • Baseline (Sample data, index at 30-Sep-26): 3 of 30 reports (10%): 08 Work orders by tech, 12 Tender pipeline, 25 Rep targets. 17 different combinations in use. 12 reports have n/a in at least one field. 14 of 30 use ship date at all. 22 of 30 refreshed in the last 30 days.
  • Evidence label: Working Hypothesis (the agreed set is open, and the index is self-reported).
  • What it tells Adam: how far the reports are from one rule, if the draft rule is chosen. It shows the scale of the clean-up: 17 combinations is nowhere near one agreed set.
  • What it doesn't tell Adam: whether any report's numbers actually agree. A report can say "ship date" and still be wrong.
  • How it could mislead: (1) If finance picks a different agreed set (for example invoice date), the 3 changes. Each alternative candidate gives 1 of 30. (2) n/a reports might not carry sales numbers at all, so 3 of 30 may understate the real position. (3) Retiring reports lifts the percentage without fixing anything. (4) Owners can relabel the index.
  • Recommendation: show as a stand-in labelled "against a draft set". Don't set a target until finance defines the set.

Our price against the winning price on lost tenders (K4.1)

  • Why it can't be built now: winning price is recorded on 5 of 25 lost tenders (20%), and 2 of the 3 big losses.
  • Substitute (plain name): Price score gap on lost tenders.
  • Calculation: for each lost tender with a scoring table, (preferred supplier's price score - Kestrel's price score) in weighted points out of 30, across the three scoring layouts (same parser as the debrief KPI, Why we lost K4.2). Positive means Kestrel was behind on price.
  • Source fields: tender_register.scoring_table, .outcome, .estimated_value, .contract_length.
  • Baseline (Sample data, whole register): 20 of 25 lost tenders have a scoring table. Kestrel was behind on price in 11 of 20 and level or ahead in 9. Behind by 1 point or more in 5. Average gap -0.03 points, median +0.45, range -2.4 to +2.1. Big losses: Southern Metro Health Network +0.60, Western Plains LHD +1.20, Highlands Ambulance -0.90. On the same lost tenders the average gap across the other five areas is +1.32 points.
  • Cross-check against the real KPI: the 3 tenders that have both a winning price and a scoring table agree in direction (our price +1.9% and +2.1% above the winner with price score behind by 0.9 and 0.6; our price 1.5% below with the price score ahead by 0.9). Three points is too few to call it a validation.
  • Evidence label: Strong Inference (scores come from the buyer's debrief, parsed from text; the cross-check has only 3 tenders).
  • What it tells Adam: whether price was where we lost ground in the buyer's own scoring. It supports the Calculation Pack note that the three big losses were not mainly on price (challenge note C10).
  • What it doesn't tell Adam: the dollar price gap. A score is the buyer's view of price, shaped by its scoring formula, not our price against the winner's price.
  • How it could mislead: a small score gap can hide a large dollar gap, and the reverse. Five lost tenders have no table. Buyers differ in how they score price.
  • Recommendation: use as the first-pass substitute. It is a stronger stand-in than the 5 known winning prices, which would show an average of +0.9% from a sample too small to trust.

5. Assumptions and open points

  • I assumed "build properly" means calculated from a source Adam can trace and defend, so a stated figure (finance's 41%) or a self-reported index doesn't qualify.
  • I treated the real KPI's name, target and status colour as untouchable. A substitute only fills the space beside a placeholder.
  • Gary needs to draft the Business Requirements change for Adam's yes (placeholder display rule, Phase 2 mark, the stand-in text). I haven't touched them.
  • If Adam prefers no stand-in for Gross margin per cent (K1) and by product group (K1.1), because the price-side signal risks being read as margin, the alternative is the placeholder only. I lean to show it with the labels above: it gives the sales leader something real about discount pressure, and it is the part of margin the team can influence.
