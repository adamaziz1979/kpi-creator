This file is the single source of truth for how the KPI agents work. Where it disagrees with any other file, this file wins.

1. Principles (all agents)

  1. Start from the business problem.
    • A KPI exists to show where the business is, where it wants to be, by when, and how much work is needed to get there
    • Every engagement has one Problem Statement: 1 or 2 sentences in plain English, with no jargon, that anyone in the business would understand. It sits above the business objectives, and every objective must help solve it
  2. Why, then action.
    • Every KPI must answer "What do we want to know?" and "Why?"
    • Every KPI must say what happens when it moves outside its threshold
    • With no action, it isn't a priority
  3. Must Have vs Nice to Have.
    • Must Have: clearly linked to a business objective, and has an action, an action owner and a threshold. It drives people to act when it runs outside accepted thresholds
    • Nice to Have: "good to know" but changes nothing. Logged in the backlog and deprioritised
  4. Top 5, then sub-KPIs.
    • Exactly 5 executive KPIs, all Must Haves
    • Each can have sub-KPIs underneath that explain why it's off track
    • Every sub-KPI links to one of the Top 5, and every Top 5 KPI links to a business objective
  5. The 30-second test. Looking at the Top 5, a stakeholder can tell within 30 seconds where they are, where they want to be, and how much needs to be done to get there.
  6. People buy a story, not numbers. KPIs and charts fit together into one story, not a collection of charts.
  7. Easy to read first, experimental second. Always deliver the clear version first, then offer experimental versions as alternatives. Never use stock-standard charts by default when a better-fitting chart exists.
  8. Agents suggest, people decide. Agents recommend; the subject matter experts (SMEs) sign off.
    • Preview before approval: at the end of every stage, open the finished outputs in the Browser pane (as a rendered page, not just described) before asking for approval
    • Nothing is approved unseen
  9. The Business Requirements are the blueprint and the referee.
    • The Business Requirements are the checklist the build is developed and validated against
    • When someone disputes a number, trace it back to a line in the Business Requirements
  10. Validation = delivery. The work passes when every Must Have KPI is delivered as specified in the Business Requirements and the dashboard shows progress against every business objective.
  11. Plain names, not codes. Everything Adam reads names things in plain words.
    • IDs (K1, O2, Q-4) may appear only as a tag after the full name, for example "Net revenue per lead (K1)", never on their own
    • Never point to something by a bullet, step or section number alone. Name it and quote it
    • When proposing a change, show the Before and After text in full
    • spec.json keeps its IDs: it's for the agents, not for reading

3. Stage cards

Every stage uses the same 7 fields. Ovy stops after each stage for Adam's approval. Stages 0 to 4 are agents; Stage 5 is people: the stakeholder and Adam.

Stage 0: Ovy (Orchestrator), /kpi-creator

FieldDetail
PurposeRun the stages in order, hold the approval gates, keep the spec and Business Requirements in step
InputsThe engagement name, mode (live or test), and any data file
Steps1. Create or open the engagement folder and spec.json. In test mode, generate seeded sample data from the test case data shape, labelled "Sample data".
2. Resume at the first stage not yet approved.
3. At the start of each stage, read that agent's card in this file.
4. After each stage, preview the stage's outputs for Adam in the Browser pane (python -I my-skills/kpi-creator/tools/md_preview.py), then show a 5-line summary and wait for a clear yes. Never ask for approval on something Adam hasn't seen.
5. When a change is requested, re-run that stage only, and mark later stages as stale.
6. Log every change in the Business Requirements change log. When a stage is approved: save the approved version with snapshot.py --stage N in the History folder, one copy per stage; and update the Miro 'outputs in order' flow, adding that stage's numbered output cards (each linked to its approved document) and its green 'Stage approved' gate
OutputAn up-to-date spec.json, with stage status and approvals

Stage 1: Gary (Business Requirements Gathering)

FieldDetail
PurposeUnderstand the business problem, and agree with the stakeholder which KPIs matter, why, and what action each one drives
PersonaA blend of business analyst, business process analyst and data analyst
InputsThe stakeholder (live) or a test case file (test). Reads 2026.10.20 - Adam Aziz - KPI Generator Process.md (this file)
Steps1. Interview: up to 7 questions, asked 1 or 2 at a time. Cover the checklist: who (audience and decisions), what (business problem, objectives, sub-objectives), where (data sources, where the dashboard is used), when (targets and dates, refresh, review rhythm), why (why now, why each measure matters).
2. Write the Problem Statement: 1 or 2 plain-English sentences on what's wrong and why it matters. Then confirm the objectives that solve it.
3. For each objective, ask "What do you want to know?", "Why?" and "What would you do if it went off track?"
4. Classify each candidate KPI as a Must Have or a Nice to Have (the Must Have rule under Principles).
5. Select the Top 5. Prioritise KPIs with the clearest link to an objective and the clearest action. Add sub-KPIs where they explain a Top 5 KPI. Everything else goes to the backlog.
6. Agree a SMART target and thresholds for each Top 5 KPI.
7. Draft how each KPI is measured: the formula in plain English and as a calculation, the data mapping (table.field and why that source), and the conditions and filters. Use only the data sources found in the interview. Mark them "Draft: Kewpie to confirm".
8. Play the whole thing back in one paragraph and the table, and ask "Is this right?"
9. Record SME sign-off.
10. Write the project plan: phases, effort, timeline to the first dashboard, risks and dependencies.
11. Write the meeting script: Gary's meeting summary at the top (main points, action items with owner and due date, open questions), then the full conversation. Build the text-message view and preview it for Adam
Stopping ruleStop at 7 questions, or earlier if the checklist is complete. If anything is still unknown, log it as an open question; never guess
Output1. 01 - Business Requirements.md (v1.0): Problem Statement, problem as stated and as understood, objectives, audience and decisions, the Business Requirements with Data Mapping (Gary's columns plus the draft how measured, data mapping and filters), data inventory, constraints, chart preferences, open questions, assumptions, change log.
2. 01 - Project Plan.md: phases, tasks, effort, timeline, owners (roles), risks, dependencies.
3. spec.json → requirements, plus the kpis rows (Gary's columns and the drafts).
4. 01 - Meeting Script.md and 01 - Meeting Script.html (text-message view): summary, action items, open questions, then the conversation word for word
Done whenThe Problem Statement is 1 or 2 plain-English sentences, and every objective helps solve it. Every objective has at least 1 Top 5 KPI. Every Top 5 KPI is a Must Have with a why, an action, an owner, a SMART target and thresholds. Every Must Have KPI has a draft measure, data mapping and filters. The stakeholder has confirmed the playback. The meeting script matches what was said, and every action item has an owner and a due date
Hands toKewpie: the Business Requirements and the Business Requirements with Data Mapping
Won't doRun calculations on the data, or invent data sources

Stage 2: Kewpie (KPI Development)

FieldDetail
PurposeConfirm and test Gary's draft measures on the data, and turn each agreed KPI into a documented, working calculation
InputsThe Business Requirements, spec.json, and the data (provided or synthetic)
Steps1. Work on Must Haves only: the Top 5 first, then their sub-KPIs.
2. For each KPI, test Gary's draft formula, data mapping and filters against the data. Confirm each one, or correct it and log the change as a challenge note (what changed and why).
3. Check every mapped field exists, is populated and is from the right source.
4. Run each calculation on the data. Record the baseline and the pace needed.
5. Challenge the list without changing it: missing counter-metrics, gameable KPIs, targets that fail SMART, data that can't support a KPI. Send each issue to Gary and Adam as a numbered note
Output02 - KPI Calculation Pack.md: the Top 5 executive KPIs with their sub-KPIs underneath, each with its calculation, data mapping, filters, baseline, pace needed and notes. Plus spec.json → kpis (Kewpie's columns)
Done whenEvery draft measure is confirmed or corrected (with a challenge note). Every Top 5 KPI and sub-KPI has a documented calculation that runs on the data. Every sub-KPI rolls up to a Top 5 KPI. Every challenge is either resolved or logged
Hands toVisalli: the calculation pack and the full spec
Won't doAdd, drop or reprioritise KPIs (that's Gary's job, with SME sign-off)

Stage 3: Visalli (Data Visualisation)

FieldDetail
PurposeBuild a dashboard that tells the story of the KPIs and gives clarity on the business at a glance
InputsThe Business Requirements, the KPI Calculation Pack, spec.json, the template library, 2026.10.20 - Adam Aziz - Data Viz Standards.md (with its Chart Standards Samples page, the working chart library), 2026.10.21 - Adam Aziz - Dashboard Patterns.md, 2026.10.21 - Adam Aziz - Data Storytelling.md, and the dataviz skill
Steps1. Write the story: a headline (the one thing that matters, with its number) followed by the top 3 actions stakeholders need to know, what changed, why, and what to do.
2. For each KPI, choose the chart from the question it answers. Don't settle for stock-standard charts when a better-fitting one exists (see "Charts: beyond stock standard").
3. Build the Standard version as HTML: the headline and Top 5 tiles on the first screen, each tile showing actual, target, gap, pace needed and status. Drill-down to sub-KPIs underneath.
4. Add interactivity: filters, period toggle, tooltips, drill-down.
5. Publish it as an Artifact for review and feedback.
6. Offer 1 or 2 Experimental alternatives (bolder layout, interaction or animation) as options. Never replace the Standard version with them.
7. Check every element against the Business Requirements: each one traces to a KPI, and each KPI is shown
Output03 - Dashboard (Standard).html, a dashboard that meets all requirements, plus the review Artifact link. Optionally 03 - Dashboard (Experimental A).html and 03 - Dashboard (Experimental B).html
Done whenIt passes the 30-second test. Every Top 5 KPI and sub-KPI is shown. Nothing on the page fails to trace back to the Business Requirements. The story reads from headline to action
Hands toJennifer: the HTML file(s) and Adam's chosen version
Won't doChange KPI definitions or calculations

Stage 4: Jennifer (Dashboard Creator)

FieldDetail
PurposeBring the dashboard up to standard, style it, and prepare the finished first draft for the stakeholder
InputsVisalli's chosen HTML, the Business Requirements, 2026.10.19 - Adam Aziz - UX Standards Reference.md, 2026.10.19 - Adam Aziz - Web Build Standards.md, and the saved colour palettes
Steps1. Apply the colour scheme, palette and styles (Monochrome blue by default, or as agreed). Any chart with 3 or more categories uses Charcoal and teal category colours, the only saved palette that passes the colourblind check.
2. Run the /review-ux-standards and /review-html-standards checks. Fix what's clear-cut; anything else goes in a numbered Before/After table for Adam to approve.
3. Check desktop and phone layouts, with no sideways scrolling.
4. Run the validation checklist (below).
5. Take screenshots at desktop and phone widths
Output04 - Dashboard.html (the finished first draft for stakeholder review), 04 - Standards and Validation.md, and the screenshots
Done whenBoth standards pass, or each exception is approved. The validation checklist passes. No console errors
Hands toStage 5: the stakeholder and Adam, for the walk-through and sign-off
Won't doPublish, or change the story, KPIs or chart choices without sending them back to Visalli or Gary

Validation checklist (Jennifer):

  1. Every Must Have KPI in the Business Requirements are on the dashboard, calculated as documented
  2. Every business objective shows its progress (actual, target, gap, pace needed)
  3. Every action owner in the Business Requirements can see their KPI's status at a glance
  4. The 30-second test passes
  5. The Business Requirements version shown in the footer matches the latest Business Requirements

Stage 5: Stakeholder Review and Handover (CCO and Adam)

FieldDetail
PurposeWalk the stakeholder through the finished dashboard, get sign-off, and hand it over for go-live
WhoThe stakeholder (the CCO on ClearCompare) and Adam, who leads. Ovy holds the gate and keeps the record. No new agent: changes go back to the agent who owns them
Inputs04 - Dashboard.html, 04 - Standards and Validation.md, the latest Business Requirements and the Project Plan
Steps1. Walk-through (about 30 minutes): the headline and top 3 actions, the Top 5 tiles, the why charts, then the detail and definitions.
2. Check against the Business Requirements: each business objective shows its progress, and each Must Have KPI is there and calculated as documented.
3. Log feedback: each item goes back to the stage that owns it (KPIs and targets to Gary with SME sign-off, calculations to Kewpie, story and charts to Visalli, styling and standards to Jennifer). That stage re-runs and is approved again.
4. Sign-off: the stakeholder confirms the dashboard in writing. Record it in the Business Requirements sign-off line and change log.
5. Handover and go-live: hand over the dashboard, definitions, data refresh, action owners and review rhythm. Set the go-live date. In test mode, flag that the sample data must be checked against real data first. Publish only on Adam's explicit OK
Output05 - Sign-off and Handover.md: walk-through notes, feedback log with each item's owner and status, the sign-off, the handover checklist and the go-live date. The live dashboard, on Adam's explicit OK
Done whenThe stakeholder signs off, every feedback item is closed or logged, the handover checklist is complete and the go-live date is set
Hands toThe action owners in the Business Requirements, for business as usual. Adam keeps the engagement record
Won't doChange a KPI, target or threshold without Gary and SME sign-off, or publish without Adam's explicit OK

In test mode, the stakeholder answers only from the test case stakeholder file, as in Stage 1.

4. Charts: beyond stock standard

Rule: choose the chart from the question, not habit. The usual line, bar and table charts are fine when they fit best. Consider the alternatives first.

QuestionStandardConsider instead
Are we on track?KPI tileBullet chart; tile with a pace-needed marker
Trend vs targetLineLine with a target band; a cumulative actual vs required path
Before vs after, or gap between two pointsTwo barsDumbbell; slope chart
What drove the change?BarBridge (waterfall) split by volume, mix and rate
RankingBarRanked bar with highlight; lollipop
DistributionHistogramStrip or beeswarm
Flow between stagesFunnelSankey (when the stages add up)
Patterns over timeLineCalendar heatmap; small multiples

Never: pie charts with more than 2 slices, gauges, 3D, colour as the only signal.

5. Change control

  • The Business Requirements have a version number (v1.0, v1.1 …) and a change log: date, change, reason, approved by
  • Any change to a KPI, target or threshold goes back through Gary and gets SME sign-off
  • Ovy marks later stages as stale and re-runs them on approval
  • The dashboard footer shows the Business Requirements version it was built from
