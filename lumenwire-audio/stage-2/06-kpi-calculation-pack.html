Total revenue, last 12 months, against the path to A$115m (K1)

Measure status: Corrected (hardware and other revenue split). Objective: Get revenue back to A$115m within two years (O1). Owner: CEO.

  • Plain English: Revenue over the last 12 months, shown each month against a straight line from FY26 actual (A$80.6m) to A$115m by June 2028. Split hardware and software.
  • Calculation: R12M(m) = -1 x SUM(gl_monthly.amount_aud) over the 12 months to month m, for account codes 4000, 4050, 4100, 4110, 4200, 4210. path(m) = 80.6m + (115m - 80.6m) x months since Jun-26 / 24. gap = R12M - path.
  • Sources and why: gl_monthly.amount_aud, period, account_code and gl_accounts.pl_group: the ledger Marcus trusts and the board pack uses. Cross-check: erp_invoice_lines.net_amount_aud by product_family reconciles to the ledger by financial year and family, largest difference A$0.00.
  • Grain: month by account (summed over cost centres).
  • Filters: pl_group 'revenue' (6 accounts); credits flipped to positive; volume rebates (4050, debit) reduce revenue and freight recovered (4210) is included. This reproduces A$118.4m (FY25) and A$80.6m (FY26) exactly. Hardware = 4000 + 4050. Software = 4100 + 4110. Other = 4200 + 4210 (developer kits and freight).
  • Baseline: A$80.54m for the 12 months to Sep-26 (hardware A$54.8m, software A$25.1m). Path at Sep-26: A$84.9m. Gap -A$4.36m (-5.14%), just beyond the 5% Red line. Observed Fact. Peak was A$122.1m in 2024-06.
  • Pace needed: (A$115m - A$80.54m) / 21 months to Jun-28 = A$1.64m a month. The straight-line path itself rises A$1.43m a month. Run rate over the last 3 months: -A$0.02m a month. Off pace.
  • Counter-metric: Gross margin %, last 12 months (K3.2), baseline 76.5%. Revenue can be pulled forward with volume rebates: in FY25, 46% of hardware revenue landed in the four quarter-end months (rebates are only ever booked in those months), against 33% if spread evenly. Watch the margin and the rebate rate (6.7% of hardware sales in FY25, 4.6% in FY26).
  • Notes: The R12M contains the A$7.4m licence of Mar-26 (9% of the figure). If the rest of FY27 repeats last year month by month without that licence, the 12 months to Jun-27 would be about A$73.1m, against a path of A$97.8m (an illustration, not a forecast). Hardware has been flat for five quarters (FY26-Q1 to FY27-Q1: A$12.9m, A$14.5m, A$12.6m, A$14.4m, A$13.4m). FY27-Q1 is +3.9% on the year before, the first quarter that is not a fall. No unmapped accounts, no duplicate ledger rows.

Sub-KPIs under Total revenue: none

Gary's list has no sub-KPI under K1. The split by hardware and software is shown inside K1, and the reasons for the movement are in the Root Cause Findings.

New devices activated in the field, last 12 months against the year before (K2)

Measure status: Corrected (repeat serials and OEM backfill). Objective: Know whether we're still growing underneath the fall (O1.1). Owner: VP Sales. Pending sign-off: Head of Platform Engineering (Tomasz Wierzbicki).

  • Plain English: Devices that joined a network for the first time in the last 12 months, against the 12 months before.
  • Calculation: keep the earliest first_seen_ts per device_serial; activations(R12M) = 10 x COUNT(device_serial) where that first date is in the 12 months to Sep-26; growth = this / prior 12 months - 1. The table is a 1-in-10 sample, so counts are multiplied by 10 (growth % is unaffected).
  • Sources and why: device_activations.device_serial, first_seen_ts (engineering telemetry, the source Marcus trusts most); oem_id, country_from_ip, module_sku for the splits.
  • Grain: device (first activation).
  • Filters: one row per serial (5,040 repeat rows dropped, 0.42% of rows); beta-firmware rows are all from 2021-22 and none fall in the window (0); data starts Jan-21; 107,210 rows have no oem_id (8.9%): 22,024 are recovered through manufacturer_code (each code maps to one OEM) and 85,186 stay unassigned. This affects only the OEM split, not the headline.
  • Baseline: -1.4% for the 12 months to Sep-26 (2,492,460 against 2,528,350). The year before was +3.0%. Observed Fact on the sample. Last quarter alone (Jul to Sep 26 against the same months of 2025): +4.9%.
  • Pace needed: (+5.0% - (-1.4%)) / 9 months to Jun-27 = +0.71 pts a month. Run rate: +0.63 pts a month. Slightly off pace, and closing.
  • Counter-metric: Share of activations that are repeat serials (a device re-joining counted as new), baseline 0.51% in the 12 months to Sep-26. It guards against re-flashed or test devices inflating the count.
  • Notes: Coverage is the open question (Q-4): the sample cannot say what share of installed devices phone home. Telemetry is a clue for end-customer demand, not yet proof. In the data, units invoiced per device activated were 1.91 in Jan to Jun 25 and 1.04 since Jul-25 (see the Root Cause Findings for what that implies). Duplicate-serial share is steady (0.47% before the dip, 0.53% during).

Sub-KPI under New devices activated: Months of stock cover at the OEMs that report (K2.1)

Measure status: Corrected (divisor chosen: units invoiced; reels, free-text names, the number of reporters). Owner: VP Sales. Pending the Head of Platform Engineering and VP Sales.

  • Plain English: Units of our parts the reporting OEMs hold, divided by the average monthly units we invoiced to those same OEMs in the same quarter. Units on hand are shown alongside.
  • Calculation: cover(q) = SUM(units on hand at the OEMs that reported in q) / (SUM(qty invoiced to those OEMs in the 3 months of q) / 3), where units = units_on_hand x 500 for reel rows, otherwise units_on_hand. Calendar quarters.
  • Divisor: units invoiced, not units activated. Same unit as the stock (parts, not finished devices). Full scale, with an OEM on every hardware line, where activations are a 1-in-10 sample with 7% of devices unassigned to an OEM. Stock is also what OEMs bought from us, so cover against purchases says how long they could stop buying. For comparison, units activated give 9.9 months in 2026-Q3 against 10.9 on invoices.
  • Sources and why: oem_inventory_reports.oem_name, quarter, sku_or_description, units_on_hand (Sales; the only stock view we have); oem_master.oem_name to match names; erp_invoice_lines.qty, oem_id, product_family (chip, module, card) for the divisor.
  • Grain: OEM by quarter.
  • Filters: 235 spellings of OEM names clean up to 35 OEMs, all matched to oem_master. 88 rows are reels of 500 (converted). 229 rows are finished goods containing our parts: kept, noted. Only the OEMs that reported in the quarter go into both the stock and the divisor, so a quarter with fewer reporters is not flattered.
  • Baseline: 10.9 months in 2026-Q3 (841,883 units at 28 OEMs, buying 77,005 a month). Peak 15.4 months in 2025-Q3. Observed Fact for the reporters. Status: Red (6 months green, over 9 months red). Gary's draft quoted 9.3 months, my first estimate using every OEM that ever reported; the quarter's own reporters give 10.9.
  • Pace needed: (6 - 10.9) / 3 quarters to Jun-27 = -1.6 months a quarter. Run rate (last 3 quarters): -0.7 months a quarter. Off pace, though improving.
  • Counter-metric: not required for a sub-KPI. Reporting is voluntary and uneven (22 to 31 OEMs a quarter), so always show how many OEMs reported.
  • Notes: Units on hand alone: 841,883 in 2026-Q3, 45% below the 2025-Q2 peak like for like. Cover fell because stock fell and purchases stayed low. Coverage is 46% of FY26 hardware revenue. Notes on the reports vary (includes goods in transit: 163, excludes Mexico plant: 162, estimate: 157, as at month end: 123), so definitions differ by OEM.

Software revenue growth without one-off large licences, last 12 months against the year before (K3)

Measure status: Corrected (the one-off exclusion is now in the formula). Objective: Keep software growing, with gross margin holding up (O2). Owner: CEO.

  • Plain English: Software revenue over the last 12 months divided by the 12 months before, minus 1, leaving out any single licence worth more than A$1m (threshold to agree with the CFO). Growth including those licences is shown alongside.
  • Calculation: (SUM(software revenue, R12M) - SUM(licences over A$1m started in R12M)) / (SUM(software revenue, prior R12M) - SUM(licences over A$1m started in prior R12M)) - 1. Software revenue = -1 x SUM(gl_monthly.amount_aud) for accounts 4100 and 4110. The licences left out come from software_licences.contract_value_aud by start_date.
  • Sources and why: gl_monthly (the P&L); software_licences to find the large licences. The licence table equals the ledger within A$27k in any month and A$13k over FY26, so removing a licence at its contract value removes it from the ledger month it was booked in. The ledger books software at the licence start date, for annual subscriptions as well as perpetual licences.
  • Grain: month by account. Filters: accounts 4100 and 4110; sign flipped; latest complete month Sep-26; single licences over A$1m left out of both windows.
  • Baseline: -15.6% for the 12 months to Sep-26 (A$17.7m against A$21.0m). 1 licence over A$1m in the current window (A$7.4m, signed 2026-03-27) and 0 in the prior window. Including it: +19.7%. FY26 on the same rule is -1.6% (headline FY26 growth was +38.2%, reproducing Marcus's 38%). Observed Fact. Status: Red (0% green, -10% red line).
  • Pace needed: (0% - (-15.6%)) / 9 months to Jun-27 = +1.7 pts a month. Run rate (last 3 months): -4.7 pts a month (-1.6%, -19.8%, -16.6%, -15.6%). Off pace, and going the wrong way. Software without the licence runs at A$1.48m a month now.
  • Counter-metric: Renewal rate of annual subscriptions due in the last 12 months (renewed / (renewed + lapsed)), baseline 88.7% (328 contracts decided; 75 still pending and 88 with no status are left out). Prior 12 months: 88.2%.
  • Notes: The licence table has 1,750 of 3,096 contracts with no oem_id. The A$7.4m contract has 0 seats (the only contract in the table with none), is a perpetual licence, and belongs to Corvane (OEM0079), which was our largest hardware customer in FY25 (A$5.4m) and bought 53% less in FY26. What the contract is, is a question for the CFO. This pack is internal; do not name the customer on the board page (Q-7). The next largest licence in the window is A$864k, so any cut-off between A$0.9m and A$7.4m gives the same answer.

Sub-KPI under Software revenue growth: Share of software revenue from the largest licences (K3.1)

Measure status: Confirmed. Owner: VP Sales.

  • Plain English: The largest licence (and the largest five) as a % of all software contract value started in the last 12 months.
  • Calculation: MAX(contract_value_aud) / SUM(contract_value_aud) for software_licences with start_date in the 12 months to Sep-26. Top 5 likewise.
  • Sources and why: software_licences.contract_value_aud, start_date, customer_name, licence_type (the contract detail Finance holds).
  • Grain: licence contract. Filters: start date in the last 12 months (617 contracts). Customer names stay off the board page (Q-7).
  • Baseline: largest licence 29.4% of A$25.2m started; top 5 40.5%. Without the Mar-26 licence: largest 4.9%, top 5 18.4%. The year before: 3.9% and 14.6%. In no earlier financial year did the largest licence exceed 6.6%. Observed Fact. Status: Red (10% green line, 20% red line).
  • Pace needed: target is below 10% by Jun-27: -19.4 pts. This happens on its own in Mar-27 when the licence leaves the 12-month window, so the figure will not show whether sales behaviour changed. Run rate: not meaningful (one contract).
  • Counter-metric: not required for a sub-KPI.
  • Notes: Even the second-largest licence is A$864k, so the cliff is one contract.

Sub-KPI under Software revenue growth: Gross margin %, last 12 months (K3.2)

Measure status: Confirmed. Owner: CFO.

  • Plain English: (Revenue minus cost of sales) divided by revenue, over the last 12 months.
  • Calculation: (R12M revenue - R12M cost_of_sales) / R12M revenue from gl_monthly by gl_accounts.pl_group (revenue accounts 4000 to 4210; cost of sales 5000 to 5100).
  • Sources and why: gl_monthly, gl_accounts (the P&L). Grain: month by account. Filters: revenue sign flipped; all five cost-of-sales accounts included.
  • Baseline: 76.5% for the 12 months to Sep-26 (72.3% the 12 months before). FY25 and FY26 reproduce Marcus's 71% and 77%. Observed Fact. Without the Mar-26 licence: 74.1% (assuming the licence carries no cost of sales; at the normal software rate of 4.5% the answer moves by under 0.5 pts). Status: Green (76% green, 74% red line).
  • Pace needed: the target is now 76% (the board's FY27 figure), and 76.5% already meets it, so no lift is needed (-0.06 pts a month). Run rate: -0.17 pts a month. Without the Mar-26 licence margin is 74.1%, which reads Amber; the figure falls to that in Mar-27 when the licence leaves the window, unless a similar deal lands.
  • Counter-metric: not required for a sub-KPI.
  • Notes: Hardware margin is 67.9% (from 66.8%); software 95.5%. Software is 31% of revenue (from 19%). Margin rises because the mix moved to software, not because hardware got cheaper to make.

Design wins that ship, as a % of design wins old enough to have shipped (K4)

Measure status: Corrected (dates required, shipped defined by the date, window kept at 18 months for the CFO and VP Sales to confirm). Objective: Keep design wins turning into shipping products (O3). Owner: VP Sales.

  • Plain English: Of the opportunities that reached design win at least 18 months ago, the share that have since reached shipping.
  • Calculation: COUNT(opp WHERE stage_entered_shipping is not blank) / COUNT(opp WHERE stage_entered_design_win <= as-of date - 18 months), among opportunities with a design-win date. Lost after design win counts as not shipped.
  • Sources and why: crm_opportunities.stage_entered_design_win, stage_entered_shipping, stage_entered_lost (the CRM is where design wins are logged). Splits: owner, region, oem_id.
  • Grain: opportunity (one OEM product).
  • Filters: design-win date on or before 2025-03-30 (18 months before the data ends). Cohort runs 2023-01-01 to 2025-03-30. 2,932 of 8,990 CRM rows have no stage dates at all (all last edited before 2023): 1,914 of them sit at design win, pre-production or shipping, so they cannot be placed in the cohort. 349 opportunities have a shipping date but no design-win date: counted separately, not in the rate. The 'confirm shipping against invoices' cross-check in the draft cannot be done: the CRM has a product name and the ERP has a SKU, with no key between them.
  • Baseline: 31.5% (672 shipped of 2,131). 7.1% were lost and 61.4% (1,308) are neither shipped nor lost. Working Hypothesis, because 33% of design-win-or-later records have no dates. Sensitivity: counting the undated records by their current stage gives 29.7%, so the result holds. Status: Amber (35% green, 30% red line).
  • Pace needed: (35% - 31.5%) / 9 months to Jun-27 = +0.39 pts a month. Run rate (the same measure as at Dec-25, Mar-26, Jun-26, Sep-26: 30.8%, 31.4%, 31.7%, 31.5%): +0.08 pts a month. Off pace, but close: the target is 3.5 pts away.
  • Counter-metric: Share of design wins later lost, baseline 7.1% of the 18-month cohort. This is the clawback test in numbers: reps are paid A$400 per design win with no clawback, so a rising lost share would show wins logged to earn the bonus.
  • Notes: Stage dates start in Jan-23, so there is no history before that. Shipped share by age for the Jan to Jun 2023 design wins (430 opportunities): 12 months 7%, 18 months 21%, 24 months 27%, 36 months 32%, 42 months 32%. The curve levels off near a third, so a longer window would not reach 80%. Q-11 (how long a design win should take to ship) is still open.

Sub-KPI under Design wins that ship: Months from design win to first shipment (K4.1)

Measure status: Corrected (only cohorts old enough). Owner: Head of Platform Engineering.

  • Plain English: The median number of months from design win to first shipment, for design wins that shipped, grouped by the quarter of the design win.
  • Calculation: MEDIAN(stage_entered_shipping - stage_entered_design_win) in months (days / 30.44), for cohorts at least 18 months old. Younger cohorts are left out because only the fastest have shipped, which makes their median look short.
  • Sources and why: crm_opportunities stage dates. Grain: opportunity. Filters: both dates present; grouped by financial quarter of the design win.
  • Baseline: median 15.0 months (middle half 11.9 to 18.9 months, 672 shipped). Including the young cohorts it would read 14.3. Observed Fact on dated records.
  • Pace needed: none: target 'to be agreed after the baseline'. Run rate: -0.6 months per cohort over the last 3 cohorts (shorter, but those cohorts are the least mature, so this is not yet a real improvement).
  • Counter-metric: not required for a sub-KPI. Notes: by design-win quarter, the median ranges between 13.3 and 16.2 months.

Sub-KPI under Design wins that ship: Design wins per quarter (K4.2)

Measure status: Confirmed. Owner: VP Sales.

  • Plain English: Count of opportunities entering design win in the quarter. Calculation: COUNT(crm_opportunities WHERE stage_entered_design_win in the financial quarter).
  • Sources and why: crm_opportunities.stage_entered_design_win. Grain: opportunity, counted once. Filters: financial quarters (July to June).
  • Baseline: FY27-Q1 260 design wins; FY26 1,180 (reproduces Marcus's 1,180), FY25 1,020, FY24 950. Observed Fact.
  • Pace needed: none: no standalone target (read with Design wins that ship). Run rate: +4.3 design wins a quarter over the last 3 full quarters. Rising count with a flat shipping rate is the pattern to watch.
  • Counter-metric: not required for a sub-KPI. Notes: reps earn A$400 per design win, with no clawback (FY25 onward). At 1,180 design wins, that is about A$472k for FY26, assuming every dated win was paid (Strong Inference).

Forecast accuracy: actual quarterly revenue against the forecast at the start of the quarter (K5)

Measure status: Corrected (units, snapshot rule, family mapping, signed error). Objective: Get revenue back to A$115m within two years (O1). Owner: CFO. Pending sign-off: CFO (Priya Anand).

  • Plain English: For each quarter, the gap between actual revenue and the forecast made at the start of that quarter, as a % of actual. Shown for Finance and for Sales, with the direction of the miss (positive = forecast too high).
  • Calculation: error = (forecast - actual) / actual; accuracy = ABS(error). forecast = sum of the three product families (hardware, software, other) from the latest snapshot on or before the first day of the quarter, converted to AUD. actual = ledger revenue for the same families and quarter.
  • Sources and why: forecast_snapshots.forecast_amount, amount_unit, source, forecast_quarter, snapshot_date, product_family (the forecasts as submitted); gl_monthly.amount_aud (actual).
  • Grain: quarter by source by product family.
  • Filters: 12 rows in AUD thousands multiplied by 1,000 (Finance, FY24 quarters). Snapshots sit 2 to 5 days before each quarter starts, so the rule 'on or before the first day' picks one clean snapshot per quarter. One Sales revision dated after the quarter began (13-Oct-25, FY26-Q2) is ignored; its effect is not tested here. The Sales snapshot for FY25-Q3 is missing (sales_crm FY25-Q3): that quarter is excluded for Sales and counted. Family mapping follows the board plan: hardware = 4000 + 4050, software = 4100 + 4110, other = 4200 + 4210 (the plan reproduces FY26 x 1.15 line by line on this mapping). 13 complete quarters: FY24-Q1 to FY27-Q1.
  • Baseline: FY27-Q1 (Jul to Sep 26): Finance forecast A$16.1m, Sales A$24.4m, actual A$19.4m. Errors: Finance -17.2%, Sales +25.6%. Average absolute miss over the last 4 quarters: Finance 23.2%, Sales 34.3%. Over all quarters: Finance 18.7% (2 of 13 quarters within 5%), Sales 22.8% (0 of 12). Observed Fact. Status: Amber for Finance and Red for Sales (10% green, 20% red line).
  • Pace needed: within 10% by FY27-Q4 (3 more quarters): Finance -2.4 pts a quarter from 17.2%; Sales -5.2 pts a quarter from 25.6%. Run rate (change in the miss over the last 3 quarters): Finance -11.3 pts a quarter, Sales -4.5 pts a quarter. The quarterly miss is noisy (Finance: FY26-Q2 51.0%, FY26-Q3 8.1%, FY26-Q4 16.7%, FY27-Q1 17.2%), so one good quarter would not show control.
  • Counter-metric: Signed bias (average forecast minus actual, as a % of actual) over the last 4 quarters: Finance +14.6%, Sales +34.3%. It stops a forecast looking accurate by being low-balled or padded in alternate quarters.
  • Notes: Which forecast the CFO wants measured is still open (Q-5). Quarters where both forecasts exist: Finance 18.6% average miss, Sales 22.8%, and their simple average 17.8%. A rule that says 'next quarter equals last quarter' would have averaged 12.0% against Finance's 18.7%.

4. Root Cause Findings

All five Top 5 KPIs are off target or have moved, so each one has a case file. Method: clues, suspects, tests, ruled out, verdict, solution options. Every test is in 02_calculations.py with its result in 02_results.json. Evidence labels: Observed Fact, Strong Inference, Working Hypothesis. Solution options are suggestions for Gary, the action owner and the subject matter expert. They are not actions or targets.

Case 1: Total revenue, last 12 months, against the path to A$115m (K1)

Clues. The 12-month figure fell from A$122.1m (2024-06) to A$80.5m. It did not slide: hardware revenue halved in one step, from A$25.7m in FY25-Q4 (Apr to Jun 25) to A$12.9m in FY26-Q1 (-50%), then stayed flat for five quarters at A$12.6m to A$14.5m. Software added one A$7.4m licence in Mar-26. The fall is in units: OEMs bought 47% fewer in FY26 (4,883,606 to 2,590,114), at an average price 0.9% higher.

Timing check. Quoted lead times fell from about 48 weeks to about 8 between Sep-23 and Jun-24. Orders then collapsed (27,327 units a month in Oct-23 to Jun-24 against 400,190 before) while invoices carried on from the order book. Orders came back at about twice installs (412,052 a month against 210,839) from Jul-24 to Apr-25, and revenue held until Jun-25. From May-25 orders matched installs (214,392 against 207,237), and the revenue step arrived in Jul-25. The cause (OEMs buying more than their installs) came before the effect (revenue stepping down when they stopped).

Verdict.

  1. Hardware sell-in fell to match installs: about A$48.0m of a A$49.8m hardware volume fall. The bridge adds up to the A$37.8m fall with A$0.0m unexplained. Bridge arithmetic: Observed Fact. The reading that OEMs bought ahead of need for two years and have now stopped: Strong Inference (timing fits and the other suspects are ruled out, but we cannot see the OEMs' own reasons).
  2. End-customer demand: A$1.8m. Real but small. Observed Fact on the sample, with coverage of the telemetry unconfirmed (Q-4).
  3. Two offsets hid part of the fall: the Mar-26 licence (A$7.4m) and smaller rebates (A$4.5m).
  4. How big the stock overhang was is a Working Hypothesis. Units invoiced exceeded units activated by about 6.9 million from Jul-22 to Jun-25. The 35 reporting OEMs held at most 1.6 million at the peak and cover 46% of hardware revenue. Either the other OEMs hold a lot, or the telemetry sees only part of the installed base. The data cannot separate the two.

What it means for the path. Since Jul-25 invoiced units sit close to installs (1.04x), so hardware is no longer being pulled down by a sell-in gap. If installs stay flat, hardware revenue stays near A$13.4m a quarter. Getting back to A$115m then needs growth that no current measure shows. Stock reports still show 10.9 months of cover at the reporting OEMs, so further destocking is possible.

Data that would settle the open parts. The Head of Platform Engineering on telemetry coverage and the share of devices that phone home (Q-4). Stock reports from all Tier A and B OEMs, not 35 voluntary ones (VP Sales). The rebate scheme terms by quarter (CFO). Why OEMs ordered at twice installs from Jul-24 to Apr-25: a direct question to the top 10 OEMs.

Solution options (for the CEO, CFO and VP Sales to weigh).

  1. Build the hardware forecast and plan from installs and OEM stock rather than last year's invoices (moves Total revenue K1 and Forecast accuracy K5).
  2. Ask the top 10 OEMs for monthly stock and their own sell-through, to see whether the overhang is gone (moves OEM stock on hand K2.1 and Total revenue K1).
  3. Review how volume rebates are tiered, because buying piled into quarter-end months (moves Total revenue K1 and its margin counter-metric).
