1. The story, in plain sentences (written before any chart)

Beginning (headline and actions)

  • Revenue over the last 12 months is A$80.5m, 5.1% below the path to A$115m.
  • Most of the fall is OEMs cutting their buying by 47% after two years of buying ahead, and the only software growth is one A$7.4m licence.
  • Hardware has held at A$12.6m to A$14.5m a quarter for five quarters: the fall has stopped, but nothing has replaced it. Without the licence, software is down 15.6%, and 31.5% of design wins have shipped against a 35% target. The business isn't yet growing underneath the fall.
  • Top 3 actions: the CEO and CFO rebuild the forecast and sales plan (A$115m by 30-Jun-28); the CEO checks the product and sales effort behind software (0% or more by 30-Jun-27); the VP Sales refocuses on design wins that will ship (35% by 30-Jun-27).

Middle (what changed, and why)

  • Revenue peaked at A$122.1m in Jun-24 and has been flat at about A$80.5m since Jun-26, while the path rises A$1.43m a month.
  • From FY25 to FY26, OEMs buying fewer units than they installed took A$48.0m off revenue. Installs falling 3.3% took only A$1.8m. The licence (A$7.4m) and smaller rebates (A$4.5m) softened the fall.
  • OEMs ordered about twice what end customers installed from Jul-24 to Apr-25, once lead times had fallen from about 48 weeks to 8. Since May-25 orders match installs (1.03 times). Strong Inference.
  • Software grew only because of the one licence: without it, software fell from A$21.0m to A$17.7m.
  • About a third of design wins ship, and the share stops rising after three years. The 80% belief was never measured.

End (what to do)

  • Three owners, three targets, from the Business Requirements. Six open decisions the board is waiting on, each with an owner and a due date.
  • New devices activated (signed off by the Head of Platform Engineering) and Forecast accuracy (signed off by the CFO) are rated on page one from Business Requirements v1.2. End customers kept installing while OEM buying fell, and neither forecast saw it.

Read-aloud test: the four beats make sense without the charts. Every cause named is in Kewpie's Root Cause Findings; none goes beyond them.

2. Pattern

Newspaper (Pattern 5), because the audience is the chair and the board, monthly, in the board pack and on screen: they read rather than scan.

PageContents
1. Front pageMasthead, headline, standfirst, the top 3 action cards, "In brief" with all of the Top 5, each rated (Forecast accuracy shows Finance and Sales on the one tile)
2. What changedLead article: revenue against the path to A$115m. Second article: the FY25 to FY26 bridge. Pull quote: A$48.0m
3. WhyThree articles, largest cause first: OEM buying against installs; the software bridge; how many design wins ship
4. What to doThe 3 actions in full (pace needed and trend), and the decisions the board is waiting on
5. Demand and forecastsNew devices activated, Months of stock cover, Forecast accuracy (signed off 27-Oct-26)
6. Back pageDrill-down (collapsed): software sub-KPIs with and without the licence; design wins per quarter and months to ship. Definitions. Business Requirements version

Page turns: Previous and Next buttons, page counter, arrow keys, swipe. Each page has its own link (#front, #changed, #why, #do, #demand, #back). Print shows every page in order.

Signed off (Business Requirements v1.2): New devices activated (K2) and Forecast accuracy (K5) are now full rated tiles on page one. The earlier "held for sign-off" tiles are gone (see the v1.2 update below).

4. Side-summary cards, in reading order

  1. Revenue for the last 12 months is A$80.5m, A$4.36m below the path to A$115m.
  2. OEMs buying fewer units than they installed took A$48.0m off revenue.
  3. OEMs ordered about twice what end customers installed, then stopped.
  4. Software grew to A$25.1m only because of one A$7.4m licence.
  5. About a third of design wins ship, and the share stops rising after three years.
  6. Three owners, three targets, and six decisions that unlock them.
  7. End customers kept installing; it was OEM buying that fell, and neither forecast saw it.
  8. Installs fell 1.4% over 12 months, but the latest quarter is up 4.9%.
  9. Reporting OEMs hold 10.9 months of stock, down from 15.4 at the peak.
  10. Neither forecast called the hardware step, and Sales has been too high in 12 of 12 quarters.
  11. One licence is 29.4% of new software contract value; without it the largest is 4.9%.
  12. Design wins rose to 1,180 in FY26 while the share that ships stayed flat.

Full card text is on the page. Each card picks up from the one before.
