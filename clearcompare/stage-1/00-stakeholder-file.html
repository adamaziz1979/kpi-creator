Context:

  • Comparison and broking business across health insurance, home loans, energy and life insurance
  • 520 staff in Sydney, Melbourne and Manila
  • $96M commission revenue
  • About 1.4M leads a year, worked by a sales team of 180 agents onshore and offshore

Stakeholder: Priya, Chief Commercial Officer. Sharp, commercial, wants answers in the Monday exec meeting.

Stakeholder file

  • Problem as stated: "Revenue per lead dropped 9% last quarter. Marketing blames sales, sales blames lead quality, and finance says it's clawbacks. I need to know which."
  • Real problem (Gary should find this): nobody can split a revenue movement into its drivers. The 9% drop is mostly mix: energy leads grew fast and pay a lower commission. Clawbacks are a smaller second cause. Today each team argues from its own report
  • Objectives:
    • O1: grow net commission revenue profitably
    • O2: convert leads more efficiently
    • O3: explain performance changes within a day, not a week
  • Sub-objectives (only if asked):
    • Cut cost per acquisition on paid search
    • Lift health insurance conversion back to 11% (now 9.4%)
    • Reduce 90-day clawbacks
  • Commission rules (only if asked about revenue):
    • Partners claw back commission if a health or life policy is cancelled within 90 days
    • Reporting today shows gross revenue
  • Audience:
    • CCO and exec team, weekly
    • Vertical heads and marketing, daily
  • Constraints:
    • Financial services: complaints and compliance breaches must stay visible
    • Agent-level data is fine for team leaders but not on the exec view
    • The company uses Tableau and Snowflake
  • Chart likes and dislikes: "Show me a bridge from last quarter to this quarter. I hate funnels that don't add up."
  • Success: "When a number moves, the dashboard tells me why before anyone asks."
