Garry's meeting summary

Main points

  • The real problem: net revenue per lead fell about 9% last quarter, and nobody can split the fall into its causes. Marketing, sales and finance each argue from their own report
  • Three objectives: grow net commission revenue profitably; convert leads more efficiently; explain performance changes within a day, not a week
  • One firm target: health insurance conversion back to 11% (now 9.4%). No dates given
  • Revenue must be net: partners claw back commission on health and life policies cancelled within 90 days. Today's reporting shows gross revenue, which hides this
  • Two audiences: the CCO and exec team weekly, at the Monday exec meeting; vertical heads and marketing daily
  • Data: leads, contacts, sales with clawbacks, marketing spend and complaints, 24 months each, in Snowflake, shown in Tableau. 6% of sales can't be matched to a lead; Manila call outcomes are missing before Mar-25
  • Must haves on the page: complaints stay visible; no agent-level data on the exec view; a bridge from last quarter to this quarter that adds up
  • Top 5 measures proposed: net revenue per lead, cost per acquisition by channel, 90-day clawback rate, lead-to-sale conversion by product area, speed to contact
  • Confirmed by Priya: the problem and the objectives. Not confirmed: every owner, action, date and target except health at 11%. These are Garry's proposals

1. Opening (00:00 to 02:00)

Garry: Thanks for the time, Priya. I'm Garry. My job today is to understand the business problem, then agree with you which measures matter, why, and what someone does when one goes off track. I'll ask up to 7 questions, one or two at a time. At the end I'll play it all back and you tell me if I've got it right.

Priya: Fine. I've got 30 minutes. I want something I can use in the Monday exec meeting.

Garry: Understood. Anything I don't hear today I'll log as an open question rather than guess.

↑ Back to top

2. The problem (02:00 to 06:00)

Garry (Q1, Why / What): What's the business problem, and why does it matter now?

Priya: Revenue per lead dropped 9% last quarter. Marketing blames sales, sales blames lead quality, and finance says it's clawbacks. I need to know which.

(Garry: three competing explanations, three teams, no shared answer. The stated problem is "revenue per lead fell". The real problem looks like "nobody can split a movement into its causes". Check this in the playback, don't lead the witness.)

Garry (probe): What happens if nothing changes in the next 6 months?

Priya: I don't know.

(Garry: no cost-of-inaction figure. Log as open. Move on.)

✅ What: problem as stated captured

↑ Back to top

3. Outcomes and numbers (06:00 to 10:00)

Garry (Q2, What): What are the 2 or 3 outcomes the business is trying to achieve? And how would you know each one is happening: any numbers in mind?

Priya: Three things:

  • Grow net commission revenue profitably
  • Convert leads more efficiently
  • Explain performance changes within a day, not a week

Priya: On numbers: cut cost per acquisition on paid search, lift health insurance conversion back to 11% (it's 9.4% now), and reduce 90-day clawbacks.

(Garry: the probe surfaced the three sub-objectives. Only one has a target value: health conversion at 11%. "Net" in the first objective matters: today's reporting may be gross. Ask about revenue rules in Q4.)

Garry: So the three objectives, with sub-objectives:

  • Grow net commission revenue profitably: cut paid search cost per acquisition, reduce 90-day clawbacks
  • Convert leads more efficiently: health conversion back to 11%
  • Explain performance changes within a day: no sub-objective

Have I grouped those correctly?

Priya: Yes.

✅ What: 3 objectives and 3 sub-objectives

↑ Back to top

4. Audience and decisions (10:00 to 13:00)

Garry (Q3, Who): Who will use this, and what decisions will they make with it?

Priya:

  • Me and the exec team, weekly, in the Monday exec meeting
  • Vertical heads and marketing, daily

Garry (probe): What format: a screen in the meeting, on a phone, or a printed board pack?

Priya: I don't know.

(Garry: two audiences, two rhythms. Exec view weekly, operational view daily. Format unknown: open question. Decisions are implied, not stated: exec decides where to push spend and sales effort; vertical heads and marketing make daily channel and team changes. Label as Strong Inference.)

✅ Who: audience and rhythm · ⚠️ format open

5. What they want to know, and what they'd do (13:00 to 18:00)

(Garry: the most valuable question. Ask it per outcome.)

Garry (Q4, Why / Action): Take each outcome in turn. What do you want to know, and what would you do if it went off track?

Priya: When a number moves, the dashboard tells me why before anyone asks.

Garry: On revenue specifically: how is commission earned and reported today?

Priya: Partners claw back commission if a health or life policy is cancelled within 90 days. Reporting today shows gross revenue.

(Garry: this is the "only if asked" commission rule. It explains why finance blames clawbacks, and why gross reporting hides it. Net revenue per lead must be net of clawbacks, and the latest 90 days of clawbacks are still landing, so the current quarter is incomplete.)

Garry (probe): If revenue per lead or conversion went off track, who would act on it, and what would they do?

Priya: I don't know.

(Garry: no actions or owners given. A KPI without an action and an owner fails the Must Have test. I'll propose actions and owners as Working Hypotheses, clearly labelled, for Adam to sign off as subject matter expert. Never present them as Priya's words.)

✅ Why: why now and why each measure · ⚠️ actions and owners to be proposed

↑ Back to top

Priya: Health conversion back to 11%.

Garry: Any date for that, or targets for the other outcomes?

Priya: I don't know.

(Garry: one target value, no dates. Propose dates and FY26-based targets as Working Hypotheses. Log "target dates" as open.)

⚠️ When: one target, no dates

7. Data (20:00 to 24:00)

Garry (Q6, Where): What data exists today, and what do you trust or not trust about it?

Priya: We've got leads, contacts, sales with clawbacks, marketing spend by day and channel, and complaints. 24 months of history. It's all in Snowflake, and we use Tableau.

Garry (probe): Anything you don't trust?

Priya: 6% of sales can't be matched back to a lead, because of manual partner uploads. And Manila call outcomes were in a separate system before March 2025.

*(Garry: five tables, 24 months each. Two known gaps:

  • Unmatched sales (6%) understate conversion and can't be tied to a channel. Keep them in revenue, exclude them from conversion and cost per acquisition, and count them
  • Manila outcomes blank before Mar-25. Contact times may still be usable

Refresh frequency not stated: assume daily from the "daily" audience, label as Working Hypothesis.)*

✅ Where: sources, grain, history, known gaps

↑ Back to top

8. Constraints and chart preferences (24:00 to 26:00)

Garry (Q7, Constraints): Anything I must or mustn't do: privacy, tools, chart likes and dislikes?

Priya: We're financial services. Complaints and compliance breaches must stay visible.

Priya: Agent-level data is fine for team leaders, but not on the exec view.

Priya: And show me a bridge from last quarter to this quarter. I hate funnels that don't add up.

*(Garry:

  • Complaints must be a measure, not a footnote: complaints per 1,000 sales sits under conversion as a counterweight, so conversion isn't bought with mis-selling
  • Agent-level conversion goes to the backlog for the exec view
  • The bridge is how "explain within a day" gets met: split the change in net revenue into volume, mix, conversion and clawbacks, and the pieces must add up to the total
  • Tools: Tableau and Snowflake noted as constraints)*

✅ All 7 questions used. Checklist complete or logged as open.
